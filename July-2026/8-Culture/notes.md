# 8. CULTURE

## 8.1. Sarnath Inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage Site

**Why in News?**  
The ancient Buddhist site of Sarnath in Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List during the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee held in Busan, South Korea, in July 2026.

**About Sarnath**

- **Historical Significance:** Sarnath is the site where Lord Buddha delivered his first sermon, known as the "Turning of the Wheel of the Law" (Dharmachakra pravartana), after attaining enlightenment. This event led to the formation of the Buddhist Sangha.
- **Sacred Geography:** Known in Buddhist tradition as Rishipatana, Ishipatana, and Mrigadava.
- **Serial Property:** The inscribed property comprises two components—Chaukhandi Stupa and the Archaeological Remains of Sarnath (including Dhamekh Stupa, Dharmrajika Stupa, Mulagandhakuti Vihara, Ashokan Pillar, and associated remains).
- **Chronology:** Archaeological evidence indicates a stratified settlement spanning over 16 centuries, from the 5th–4th centuries BCE to the 12th century CE.
- **ASI Conservation:** Excavated and conserved extensively by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) for over 150 years; the ASI also manages the Sarnath Site Museum (established 1904), which houses key finds including the Mauryan Lion Pillar (now India's National Emblem) and the Gupta-period Dharmachakra-pravartana Buddha image.

**Outstanding Universal Value (UNESCO Criteria)**

- **Criterion (iii):** Bears witness to the enduring spiritual significance of Sarnath as one of the four principal destinations of Buddhist pilgrimage, demonstrated by the large number of religious buildings and monuments constructed and rebuilt over centuries.
- **Criterion (vi):** Directly associated with events of outstanding significance to Buddhism—Buddha's first sermon and the meeting of his first disciples.

**Significance**

- **Cultural Milestone:** Becomes India's 45th UNESCO World Heritage Site and Uttar Pradesh's fourth (joining the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, and Fatehpur Sikri).
- **Long Journey:** Sarnath was included in UNESCO's Tentative List in July 1998 and formally inscribed in 2026 after a rigorous nomination process.
- **Global Recognition:** Reinforces India's commitment to preserving and showcasing its civilisational heritage on the world stage.
- **Tourism & Research:** Expected to strengthen the Buddhist Circuit and promote sustainable heritage tourism.

**Way Forward**

- **Conservation Focus:** Emphasis on sustainable tourism management, prevention of unplanned development in the buffer zone, and preservation of authenticity and integrity.
- **Community Engagement:** Continued collaboration with stakeholders to balance conservation with pilgrimage and visitor needs.

*Source: UNESCO, PIB, The Hindu, The Indian Express, 25-26 Jul 2026*

## 8.2. Gyan Bharatam Mission Accelerates Manuscript Digitisation

**Why in News?**  
In July 2026, the Ministry of Culture convened the National Digitization Drive Workshop in New Delhi under the Gyan Bharatam Mission, highlighting substantial progress in surveying and digitising India's vast manuscript heritage.

**About Gyan Bharatam Mission**

- **Objective:** To survey, document, conserve, and digitise India's manuscript wealth spanning diverse knowledge systems including philosophy, Ayurveda, literature, mathematics, yoga, and other disciplines.
- **National Survey Progress:** Over 1.2 crore manuscripts have been reported nationwide. Following rigorous verification, Uttar Pradesh recorded over 30.10 lakh verified manuscripts, followed by Rajasthan with around 19 lakh.
- **District Highlights (UP):** Ayodhya (10.42 lakh), Unnao (4.57 lakh), Varanasi (4.23 lakh), among others; verification led to rejection of entries not meeting national standards.
- **Workshop Outcomes:** Brought together over 200 representatives from States/UTs, Gyan Bharatam Centres, custodians, scholars, experts, and technology partners to finalise standards for imaging, metadata, conservation, and implementation.

**Key Initiatives**

- **Unified Digital Ecosystem:** Development of a National Digital Manuscript Ecosystem supported by AI platforms and a structured access policy framework.
- **Conservation-Digitisation Synergy:** Fragile manuscripts to be conserved alongside digitisation, with funding support for infrastructure, equipment, and trained manpower.
- **Collaborative Models:** Showcased best practices from States and Centres including Rajasthan, BORI Pune, Jammu & Kashmir, Asiatic Society Kolkata, KOBA (Gujarat), Vrindavan Research Institute, Sevadhi (Kerala), and Jamia Hamdard (Delhi).
- **Next Phase:** Transition from survey/verification to high-resolution scanning and secure uploading of manuscripts to the national digital portal.

**Significance**

- **Preservation of Knowledge:** Safeguards centuries-old texts and indigenous knowledge systems for future generations.
- **Accessibility & Research:** Enables wider scholarly access while ensuring appropriate access controls for sensitive materials.
- **Capacity Building:** Strengthens institutional capacity across states and custodial institutions.
- **Civilisational Legacy:** Embodies the ethos of "jñānam paramam dhyeyam" (knowledge is the highest pursuit).

*Source: PIB, Ministry of Culture, Hindustan Times, 18 Jul 2026*

## 8.3. NEWS IN SHORTS

### 8.3.1. Jodhpuri Mojari Secures GI Tag

- **Recognition:** The nearly 200-year-old Jodhpuri Mojari leather footwear received a Geographical Indication (GI) tag in July 2026.
- **Impact:** Boosts artisan livelihoods, brand identity, and global market potential for Jodhpur's handicraft sector.
- **Context:** GI certificate handed over to Jodhpur Handicraft Exporters Association and Gram Vikas Seva Sansthan.
*Source: The Indian Express, 11-13 Jul 2026*

### 8.3.2. Karnataka Products Set for GI Recognition

- **New Applications:** NABARD extended support for GI registration of Tiptur coconut, Hoysala-style stone art, Sanikatte salt, and Kunbi Mudli (a climate-resilient taro variety).
- **Broader Efforts:** Part of ongoing initiatives to strengthen value chains, branding, marketing, and enterprise development for GI products in Karnataka.
*Source: The Hindu, 7 Jul 2026*

### 8.3.3. Meghalaya Launches Mission Golden Spice for Lakadong Turmeric

- **Project:** ₹175.45 crore convergence-led "Mission Golden Spice – Integrated Lakadong Turmeric Value Chain Enhancement Project" launched for 2025–2030.
- **USP:** Promotes GI-tagged Lakadong turmeric, known for its exceptionally high curcumin content (nearly four times the global average).
- **Objectives:** Expand cultivation, reduce post-harvest losses, strengthen processing/cold chain, enhance branding and GI monetisation, and improve farmer incomes.
*Source: PIB, MDoNER, 21 Jul 2026*

### 8.3.4. Unjha Cumin and Fennel Receive GI Tags

- **Recognition:** Unjha Cumin and Unjha Fennel (North Gujarat) awarded GI tags, certifying their distinct geographical identity and quality.
- **Expected Benefits:** Enhanced brand value, export opportunities, potential price premiums (20–30%), and higher incomes for farmers and traders.
- **Collaborative Effort:** Supported by APMC Unjha, SDAU, EDII, and State/Central agencies.
*Source: The Economic Times, 14 Jul 2026*

### 8.3.5. Madhya Pradesh Secures GI Tags for 12 Horticultural Crops

- **Milestone:** Twelve horticultural crops from Madhya Pradesh received GI recognition simultaneously—a first for any Indian state for such a cluster.
