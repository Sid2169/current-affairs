# 6. SOCIAL ISSUES

## 6.1. MAHARASHTRA GIVES LEGAL RECOGNITION TO WOMEN FARMERS

**Why in News?**
Maharashtra became the **first Indian state to legally recognise women farmers** through the **Women Farmers Empowerment Act**, granting them access to welfare, credit and institutional support **without requiring land ownership**.

**About the Act**

- Historically, agricultural benefits, extension services and formal bank loans have been legally tied to **land ownership**, which is held overwhelmingly by men.
- The Act addresses this deep gender inequality in access to land, water, credit and natural resources.

**Key Data on Women in Agriculture**

- About **73% of the rural female workforce** in India is engaged in agriculture, compared to **47% of rural men**.
- Builds on Maharashtra's **Fourth Policy for Women (2024)** and aligns with the **National Policy for Farmers (2007)**.

**Core Legal Features and Institutional Mechanisms**

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Broad definition of "farmer" | Includes women in crop husbandry, animal husbandry, poultry, fishery, agro-forestry, vermiculture, mushroom cultivation and primary processing — as owner **or** labourer. |
| Woman Farmer Certificate (WFC) | Official proof of occupation issued through the Gram Sabha or Nagar Panchayat, removing the land-title requirement for benefits. |
| Digital registry and fund | A centralised registry of certified women farmers supported by a dedicated state-level empowerment fund. |
| Two-tier oversight | A State-Level Monitoring Committee (Chief Secretary) and a Governing Council (Chief Minister) for long-term policy direction. |

**Significance**

- **Unlocks formal credit:** the WFC lets women access crop insurance and low-interest institutional loans without pledging land as collateral.
- **Targeted welfare:** the registry enables gender-responsive skilling for single, widowed and marginalised farmers.
- **Climate resilience:** strengthens women's role in sustainable practices such as vermiculture and agro-forestry.

**Challenges**
- Certification and awareness gaps in remote areas; risk of elite capture.
- Weak integration with inheritance and land-record systems.
- Funding adequacy for the empowerment fund.

**Way Forward**
- Link the registry with **Aadhaar, land records and DBT** for seamless delivery.
- Expand the model to other states and pair it with **awareness drives and digital literacy**.

*Source: Indian Express, 16 July 2026; Maharashtra Women Farmers Empowerment Act*

## 6.2. THE UNFPA DEMOGRAPHIC FUTURES SURVEY 2026

**Why in News?**
The **United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA)** released **Lives, Choices and Futures — the Demographic Futures Survey 2026**, a global study of the aspirations and family decisions of young adults.

**About the Survey**

- A cross-national study designed to examine the **aspirations, relationship patterns and family decisions** of young adults aged **18–39**.
- Sampled about **1,08,926 internet-connected young adults across 73 countries and territories**.

**Key Findings**

- **Marriage ideals persist:** over two-thirds prefer a living arrangement involving marriage — 36% favouring marriage before cohabitation and 34% after.
- **Aspiration–reality gap:** among respondents aged 25–39 who wish to marry or partner, **25% are single and not dating** (men 30%, women 19%).
- **Economics before partnership:** financial security is rated the most critical consideration (81%), with economic and housing barriers the primary obstacles (57%).
- **Preconditions for parenthood:** financial stability (88%), stable employment (87%) and emotional readiness (85%) rank highest.
- **Joy over incentives:** 80% cite the happiness children bring as the top motivation; state incentives rank among the lowest.
- **Structural barrier to family size:** economic and housing constraints are the single largest barrier globally (72%).
- **Declining achieved fertility:** across regions, the achieved number of children among older cohorts is consistently lower than the ideal reported by younger cohorts.

**India-Specific Trends**

- Women aged 18–39 desire an ideal family size of **2.1 children**, against an actual average of **1.0** among those aged 35–39.
- Indian men report an ideal of **2.2 children**, against an actual **1.1**.

**Implications for India**

- Reinforces the need for **affordable housing, childcare, stable employment and gender-equal care work**.
- Informs the "demographic dividend" debate as fertility declines below replacement level in several states.
- Suggests that **family welfare policy** must address economic precarity, not merely incentives.

*Source: UNFPA, Demographic Futures Survey 2026 (reported 20 July 2026)*

## 6.3. INDIA'S GEN Z AND UNEMPLOYMENT

**Why in News?**
Concerns over India's **Gen Z unemployment crisis** have intensified, with data highlighting skill mismatches, informality and low labour-force participation — especially among young women.

**Who is Gen Z?**
Working-age individuals aged **15 to 26 years** (born 1997–2012), representing a talent pool of roughly **29.94 crore** people.

**Key Data and Statistics**

- **Low formal wage absorption:** only **14.8% of Gen Z males** and **4.7% of Gen Z females** are regular wage earners; the rest are casual labourers, unpaid family workers or in unpaid domestic work.
- **High informality:** about **79.9%** of Gen Z workers lack any social-security coverage; only **14.1%** hold a formal employment contract.
- **Depressed LFPR:** Gen Z labour-force participation is **41.7%** (versus 75% for Millennials), falling to **28%** among rural and **21.1%** among urban young women.
- **Domestic burden on women:** about **27.1% of Gen Z females** are engaged exclusively in unpaid domestic duties, against just **0.32%** of males.

**Reasons**

- **Education–work mismatch:** credential inflation without practical, industry-relevant skills.
- **Automation and AI:** displacement of traditional entry-level white-collar and administrative roles.
- **Structural and social barriers:** lack of affordable childcare, safety and mobility constraints and unequal domestic expectations keep educated women out of paid work.

**Way Forward**
- Scale **apprenticeships, vocational training and industry-linked skilling**.
- Promote **labour-intensive manufacturing and services** to absorb young workers.
- Expand **childcare, safe transport and flexible work** to raise female participation.
- Strengthen **social security and formalisation** of gig and platform work.

*Source: PLFS / Insights, 20 July 2026*

## 6.4. THE DRAFT NATIONAL HEALTH RESEARCH POLICY, 2026

**Why in News?**
The Centre's **Department of Health Research** released the **Draft National Health Research Policy, 2026** for public feedback until **27 July 2026**.

**About the Policy**

- India's **first unified national framework** spanning biomedical science, clinical medicine, public health, epidemiology, digital health, health systems, behavioural sciences and emerging technologies.
- Treats health research as a **national problem-solving architecture** rather than isolated academic studies.

**Key Features**

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Investment boost | A **six-fold increase** in government health-research spending — from **0.024% of GDP** to **0.072% by 2037**, reaching **0.15% by 2047**. |
| Three-tier governance | A National Health Research Stewardship Committee, the Department of Health Research as nodal agency, and **ICMR** for scientific leadership. |
| Dynamic research agenda | A National Health Research Agenda prioritising **TB, AMR, cancer and child malnutrition**. |
| Impact-based evaluation | Shifts from paper counts to the **ICMR Impact of Research and Innovation Scale (ICMR-IRIS)**. |
| Decentralisation | Mandates states to frame **local research agendas** based on regional disease patterns. |
| Ethics and integrity | Streamlined multi-centre trial approvals and a **National Research Integrity Office (NRIO)**. |
| Data and technology | Emphasises data governance, cybersecurity and **responsible AI in diagnostics**. |

**Significance**

- Directs public research towards **real-world health needs** and affordable indigenous technologies, vaccines and diagnostics.
- Strengthens **state-level research** and early-warning systems for future outbreaks.
- Supports the **Viksit Bharat 2047** vision of a resilient health system.

**Challenges**
- Ensuring sustained funding and institutional capacity beyond elite institutions.
- Balancing data sharing with privacy and cybersecurity.
- Translating research into last-mile delivery.

*Source: PIB, 16 July 2026; Department of Health Research*

## 6.5. NEWS IN SHORTS

### 6.5.1. PERFORMANCE GRADING INDEX (PGI) 2.0

- The **Department of School Education & Literacy** released PGI reports for **States/UTs (PGI-S)** and **Districts (PGI-D)** for 2025–26.
- PGI 2.0 groups states and districts into **performance bands** rather than numerical league-table ranks.
- **PGI-S** evaluates all **36 States/UTs** on a **1,000-point matrix** across **70 indicators** and six domains (learning outcomes, access, infrastructure, equity, governance, teacher education).
- **PGI-D** covers **784 districts** on a **600-point matrix** across six categories.
- Originally introduced by the Ministry of Education in **2018**; top grade is **Utkarsh**.

*Source: PIB, 9 July 2026*

### 6.5.2. NANDAN NILEKANI-LED TASK FORCE ON EXAMINATION REFORMS

- The Prime Minister announced a **high-powered task force on examination reforms**, chaired by **Nandan Nilekani** (co-founder, Infosys).
- Mandate: recommend **technology-enabled and systemic reforms** to make public examinations — especially those conducted by the **National Testing Agency (NTA)** — secure, transparent and credible.
- Members include **Dr. S. Somnath** (former ISRO Chairman), **Tapan Deka** (former IB Director), **Prof. V. Kamakoti** (IIT Madras) and **Anita Karwal** (former Education Secretary).

*Source: PIB, 27 July 2026*

### 6.5.3. PUBLIC EXAMINATIONS (PREVENTION OF UNFAIR MEANS) AMENDMENT BILL, 2026

- Introduced in the **Lok Sabha**, it overhauls the **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024**.
- Empowers the Centre to constitute a **Special Task Force (STF)** to investigate paper leaks, with investigations to be completed within **2 months**.
- Mandates every State/UT to designate a **Special Fast Track Court** (a Court of Session) for day-to-day trials.
- Aims to deter **organised syndicates, service providers and coaching mafias** from compromising exam integrity.

*Source: PIB, 28 July 2026*

### 6.5.4. INDIA'S FIRST APPROVED DENGUE VACCINE (QDENGA)

- The **Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO)** granted marketing authorisation for **Qdenga®**, India's first approved dengue vaccine.
- A **live, attenuated tetravalent** vaccine using a **dengue virus type-2 backbone** with genes from the other three serotypes — protecting against all four strains (DEN-1 to DEN-4).
- Indicated for individuals aged **4–60 years**; a **two-dose** regimen given **three months apart**.
- Developed by **Takeda GmbH, Germany**; imported by Takeda Biopharmaceuticals India; has **WHO prequalification** and approvals in over 42 countries.

*Source: PIB, 22 July 2026*

### 6.5.5. CYCLOSPORIASIS

- The **US CDC** issued a health advisory following a widespread **cyclosporiasis** outbreak across **34 states**.
- Caused by **_Cyclospora cayetanensis_**, a microscopic, single-celled **parasite** — a form of food-poisoning.
- Spreads mainly through **contaminated food and water**; targets the digestive system, causing prolonged intestinal distress.
- Generally not life-threatening but can cause complications if untreated.

*Source: The Hindu, 16 July 2026*

### 6.5.6. THE KORKU COMMUNITY

- The **Korku** are a **particularly vulnerable tribal group (PVTG)** concentrated in southern Madhya Pradesh (Khandwa, Burhanpur, Betul, Chhindwara) and adjoining Maharashtra near **Melghat Tiger Reserve**.
- Historically hunter-gatherers of the **Satpura range** along the Tapti river, now settled agriculturalists practising double-cropping.
- Speak **Korku**, an endangered **Austroasiatic Munda** language written in Devanagari.
- Governed by a **Korku Panchayat** headed by a **Patel**; four sub-sects and 16 **gotras** named after natural elements.
- Known for **community-based conservation** practices.

*Source: Down To Earth, 16 July 2026*

### 6.5.7. THE DWEEPTI YOJANA

- **Chhattisgarh** launched the **DWEEPTI Yojana** (Decentralised Women Empowerment on Energy and Policy for Transformative Inclusion) — the first Indian state to adopt a **women-led distributed renewable energy policy**.
- An initiative of the state's Panchayat and Rural Development Department with **Transform Rural India (TRI)** under the **Green Economy Transition Mission**.
- Trains SHG women as **"Solar Didis"** — solar vendors, technicians, installers and entrepreneurs.
- Cluster Level Federations are registered as vendors under **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** in partnership with CSPDCL.
- Piloted in **Kabirdham district**; advances the **Lakhpati Didi** vision.

*Source: Insights, 28 July 2026*

### 6.5.8. AI & INDIA'S INFORMAL WOMEN WORKERS

- About **82% of employed women** in India work in the **informal sector**; per **PLFS 2023–24**, **76.9% of rural working women** are in agriculture.
- **Gender-responsive AI** — real-time advisory platforms, NLP and automated decision-making — can boost productivity in agriculture, domestic work and micro-enterprises.
- A 2024 study on the multilingual tool **Farmer.Chat** across 12 states found **61% of female users** reported improved quality of life within 45 days.
- Supported by digital public infrastructure such as the **IndiaAI Mission** and **BHASHINI**.
- Without inclusive design, digital expansion risks **deepening existing inequalities**.

*Source: The Hindu, 28 July 2026*

### 6.5.9. THE CORPORATE MITRA SCHEME

- A central initiative of the **Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA)** to create a certified cadre of para-professionals called **Corporate Mitras**.
- They provide accessible, affordable **compliance, accounting and advisory services** to **MSMEs**.
- Aims to reduce the regulatory and secretarial burden on MSMEs in **Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities** and enhance **Ease of Doing Business**.
- IICA Shillong organised an awareness webinar for youth, students and MSMEs in Arunachal Pradesh.

*Source: PIB, 27 July 2026*

### 6.5.10. THE ATMANIRBHAR PANCHAYAT PROGRAMME

- Launched by the **Ministry of Panchayati Raj** under the **Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan (RGSA)**, along with the **SAMARTH Panchayat Portal**.
- Helps **Gram Panchayats and Block Panchayats** identify idle local assets and untapped opportunities and convert them into **bankable, revenue-generating projects**.
- Aims to build sustainable **Own Sources of Revenue (OSR)** for Panchayati Raj Institutions, reducing fiscal dependence on grants.
- Strengthens **fiscal decentralisation and local self-governance**.

*Source: The New Indian Express, 28 July 2026*
