# 10. PLACES IN NEWS

The places in the news in July 2026 range from a newly designated UNESCO World Heritage Site and a flagship spaceport to flood- and landslide-hit districts of the Himalayas and the Northeast, a global maritime chokepoint, and an overseas city where India signed a new strategic partnership. The list below is grouped broadly by region and covers the location, the reason for the news and the significance of each place.

---

## India

### 10.1. SARNATH (Uttar Pradesh, India)
- **Location:** About 10 km north-east of Varanasi, on the left bank of the Varuna near its confluence with the Ganga, in the Gangetic plain.
- **Why in News?** On **25 July 2026**, the ancient Buddhist site of Sarnath was **designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site** (at the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee).
- **Significance:** Sarnath is where **Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon** after enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, setting in motion the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* and the "First Turning of the Wheel of Dharma" to his five disciples. It is also the site of the **Lion Capital of Ashoka (c. 250 BCE)**, which is the **State Emblem of India**. Sarnath is one of the eight most important Buddhist pilgrimage sites and, with the designation, joins India's list of World Heritage Sites.

### 10.2. NAMCHI / TEESTA RIVER (Sikkim, India)
- **Location:** Namchi district in southern Sikkim, along the **Teesta River**, a major tributary of the Brahmaputra.
- **Why in News?** On **20 July 2026**, a suspected **methane gas explosion collapsed part of an under-construction tunnel at a hydropower project** on the Teesta near Namchi, killing several workers, with others trapped; rescue teams detected toxic gases.
- **Significance:** The Teesta is central to Sikkim's hydropower potential and to the long-standing India–Bangladesh Teesta water-sharing question. The accident highlights **safety concerns in Himalayan tunnel and hydropower construction** in a seismically active, ecologically fragile zone.

### 10.3. BRAHMAPUTRA VALLEY / UPPER ASSAM (Assam, India)
- **Location:** The Brahmaputra Valley, covering districts such as Sivasagar, Charaideo, Jorhat and Golaghat in Upper Assam.
- **Why in News?** The **2026 Assam floods**, triggered by a cloudburst in neighbouring Nagaland on 19 July and unusually heavy monsoon rain, inundated Upper Assam from around **19 July 2026**, affecting hundreds of thousands. The death toll rose to 62 by 25 July and to 85 by early August.
- **Significance:** The Brahmaputra and its 50-plus tributaries make Assam one of India's most flood-prone States. The floods illustrate the role of **upstream rainfall in the Naga and Patkai Hills**, the limited drainage capacity of tributaries when the Brahmaputra runs high, and the recurring challenge of flood management and wetland/floodplain ecosystems in the Northeast.

### 10.4. WAYANAD DISTRICT (Kerala, India)
- **Location:** A hilly district in the Western Ghats in northern Kerala.
- **Why in News?** On **7 July 2026**, a massive **landslide triggered by heavy rain hit a bridge in Wayanad district**, killing two people and leaving seven missing.
- **Significance:** Wayanad is highly vulnerable to landslides and had witnessed a catastrophic landslide in 2024. The event renews focus on **Western Ghats slope stability, deforestation, unplanned construction and early-warning systems** in the region.

### 10.5. PUNE (Maharashtra, India)
- **Location:** Pune district in the rain-shadow interior of the Western Ghats, Maharashtra.
- **Why in News?** On **8 July 2026**, heavy rain caused a **garbage heap to collapse onto an administrative building at a waste-to-energy plant near Pune**, killing nine workers and prompting a temporary suspension of operations.
- **Significance:** The incident draws attention to **solid-waste management, waste-to-energy technology and worker safety** in urban Maharashtra, a state that processes a large share of India's municipal solid waste.

### 10.6. BHIWANDI (Maharashtra, India)
- **Location:** A powerloom and warehousing town in Thane district, within the Mumbai Metropolitan Region.
- **Why in News?** On **30 July 2026**, at least nine people were killed and several trapped after a **four-storey building collapsed/exploded** in Bhiwandi.
- **Significance:** Bhiwandi is a major logistics and textile hub. The collapse highlights **building-safety violations, unauthorised construction and monsoon-related structural risks** in fast-growing peri-urban settlements.

### 10.7. ANANTNAG (Jammu & Kashmir, India)
- **Location:** A district in south Kashmir in the Jhelum valley, home to the Pahalgam hill station.
- **Why in News?**
  - On **22 July 2026**, a **35-year-old head constable was shot dead** in a militant attack in Anantnag; the Resistance Front claimed responsibility.
  - On **6 July 2026**, the **NIA filed a chargesheet against LeT founder Hafiz Saeed** in connection with the **2025 Pahalgam terror attack**.
- **Significance:** South Kashmir remains a focus of counter-terror operations. The events link militancy in the region to cross-border terror networks and to India's legal and diplomatic response.

### 10.8. CHAMBA DISTRICT (Himachal Pradesh, India)
- **Location:** A district in the Shivalik and Lesser Himalaya ranges, drained by the Ravi river.
- **Why in News?** On **24 July 2026**, **thirteen people were killed** when a **heavy boulder rolled down a hill and hit moving vehicles** in Chamba district.
- **Significance:** The accident underlines **Himalayan slope hazards, rockfall and road-safety risks** on hill highways during the monsoon.

### 10.9. POLAVARAM / GODAVARI RIVER (Andhra Pradesh, India)
- **Location:** Polavaram in Eluru district on the **Godavari River**, India's largest peninsular river.
- **Why in News?** On **18 July 2026**, **five people, including three women, drowned in the Godavari while fishing** near Polavaram district.
- **Significance:** Polavaram is the site of the **Polavaram multipurpose irrigation and hydropower project**, a national project on the Godavari. The location is relevant to riverine livelihoods, dam safety and inter-State water issues on the Godavari.

### 10.10. LATEHAR (Jharkhand, India)
- **Location:** A district in the Chota Nagpur Plateau in Jharkhand, bordering the Maoist-affected belt.
- **Why in News?** On **13 July 2026**, **Ravindra Ganjhu, a top wanted CPI (Maoist) leader, was confirmed arrested** by Jharkhand Police in Latehar during an anti-Maoist operation.
- **Significance:** The arrest is a significant success in the Left-Wing Extremism (LWE) security grid and reflects the strategy of combining security operations with development in the "Red Corridor".

### 10.11. AHMEDABAD (Gujarat, India)
- **Location:** Gujarat's largest city, on the Sabarmati River.
- **Why in News?**
  - On **7 July 2026**, the **Gujarat High Court upheld the conviction of 49 accused** (38 death sentences and 11 life imprisonments) in the **2008 Ahmedabad serial bombings**.
  - On **18 July 2026**, an **unauthorised firecracker factory exploded in Ahmedabad**, killing nine people.
- **Significance:** Ahmedabad was the site of the 2008 serial blasts. The verdict affirms the judicial process against organised terror, while the factory blast highlights **industrial-safety and licensing failures**.

### 10.12. SATISH DHAWAN SPACE CENTRE, SRIHARIKOTA (Andhra Pradesh, India)
- **Location:** Sriharikota island on the Bay of Bengal coast in Andhra Pradesh.
- **Why in News?** On **18 July 2026**, **Skyroot Aerospace launched Vikram-1**, described as **India's first privately built orbital rocket**, from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, carrying small satellites into low Earth orbit.
- **Significance:** Sriharikota is ISRO's primary launch centre. The private launch signals the maturing of India's **space economy** and the role of IN-SPACe and NSIL in enabling private participation.

### 10.13. TAJ TRAPEZIUM ZONE / AGRA (Uttar Pradesh, India)
- **Location:** A roughly 10,400 sq km area around Agra and the Taj Mahal, spanning parts of Uttar Pradesh.
- **Why in News?** On **23 July 2026**, the **Supreme Court permitted the Taj Trapezium Zone Authority to process pending applications for non-polluting MSMEs** within the zone.
- **Significance:** The zone was notified to protect the Taj Mahal from air pollution. The order balances **heritage conservation with local industrial and employment interests**, a recurring environment-versus-development question.

### 10.14. NEW DELHI (India)
- **Location:** India's capital, on the Yamuna, seat of Parliament.
- **Why in News?**
  - The **2026 Jantar Mantar protests / "Sansad Chalo" march** over the NEET-UG paper leak continued through July; on **20 July 2026 riot police baton-charged protesters** marching towards Parliament House.
  - On **21 July 2026**, **Rahul Gandhi and other opposition leaders were detained** during protests; Parliament was adjourned repeatedly.
- **Significance:** The protests shaped the political agenda of the month, leading to the Union Education Minister's resignation and to Parliament passing the Anti-Paper Leak law.

## World

### 10.15. STRAIT OF HORMUZ (between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman)
- **Location:** A narrow waterway linking the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea, bounded by Iran and Oman.
- **Why in News?** Through July 2026, the **2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis** disrupted shipping, with tankers attacked and vessels seized. On **15 July 2026**, India's **DG Shipping advised against deploying Indian sailors** on vessels transiting the Strait after **two Indian seafarers were killed**; on **24 July 2026**, the LPG tanker **Disha**, carrying 28 Indian nationals, was attacked but all crew were reported safe.
- **Significance:** The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most important **oil chokepoint**, through which a large share of global crude and LNG moves. The crisis directly affects India's **energy security** and the safety of Indian seafarers.

### 10.16. ODESA (Ukraine)
- **Location:** A major Black Sea port city in southern Ukraine.
- **Why in News?** Around **19 July 2026**, a Russian strike hit a cargo ship off Odesa, **killing crew members including Indians**. On **21 July 2026**, **India summoned the Russian chargé d'affaires** over the killing of Indian seafarers (linked to the vessel *MV Golden Leo*).
- **Significance:** Odesa is central to Ukraine's grain exports via the Black Sea. The incident highlights the risks to **Indian seafarers and merchant shipping in conflict zones**, and India's balancing role in the Russia–Ukraine war.

### 10.17. ABU DHABI (United Arab Emirates)
- **Location:** Capital of the UAE, on the Persian Gulf coast.
- **Why in News?** On **2 July 2026**, **Bank of Baroda informed stock exchanges that it had paid ₹5,700 crore** to settle **NMC Health-related disputes at the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM)** courts.
- **Significance:** Abu Dhabi Global Market is an international financial centre and a common-law jurisdiction. The settlement relates to the cross-border insolvency of NMC Health and reflects the exposure of Indian public-sector banks to overseas corporate defaults.

### 10.18. AUCKLAND (New Zealand)
- **Location:** New Zealand's largest city, on the North Island.
- **Why in News?** On **11 July 2026**, during Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit, **India and New Zealand announced a strategic partnership** in Auckland.
- **Significance:** The partnership expands cooperation on defence, security, trade, culture, science and maritime issues, reflecting India's growing engagement with the Pacific as part of its Indo-Pacific strategy.
